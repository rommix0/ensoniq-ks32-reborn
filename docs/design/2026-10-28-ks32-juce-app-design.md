## 1. Intent

A standalone Windows app that is a KS-32. The user starts it, it boots OS v3.01 from their ROMs, and
they play the factory sounds from the Q25 and work the on-screen front panel the way they would work
the real instrument. The app is a thin JUCE shell around the existing `Machine`: everything the user
sees on the panel comes from the firmware, and everything they press goes to the firmware.

**Why now.** Plan 6 proved the machine headless: it boots, plays from MIDI files and live MIDI, and
the panel protocol is decoded (`docs/ks32-hardware-interface.md`). `ks32emu play` shows the panel
as text in a console. The app replaces that console with the panel itself.

## 2. Goals

1. **Real-time-safe machine.** `Machine::runFrame`, `midiIn`, `button`, `key`, `setAnalog` and
   reading the panel state do not allocate, and the MIDI receive queue is bounded (ruling F-1,
   `docs/verification-plan6.md`). This comes first, because the audio thread owns the machine.
2. **A JUCE-free engine** between the machine and the app. It owns one `Machine` and one
   `Resampler`, takes input events from the GUI and MIDI through a fixed-size lock-free queue, renders
   audio blocks at the host rate, and publishes a panel snapshot the GUI can read without locking.
   Plain C++17, in the emulation tree, tested by `ens_tests` on both toolchains.
3. **The JUCE app.** An `AudioProcessor` with the Standalone format, built with MSVC x64. It owns the
   engine. MIDI from the selected input goes to the machine's MIDI in. Audio goes to the selected
   output device at the device rate.
4. **The front-panel editor**, as the "Front panel" artboard shows it: every control at its place
   and label, the 2 x 16 display, the 15 LEDs, the sliders, the wheels and the 76-key keyboard. It is
   drawn with JUCE graphics (no images), and scales with the window.
5. **The settings page**, as the "Settings" artboard shows it, with the changes in §6.4.
6. **Battery memory** saved to a file when the app closes and loaded at start, so the firmware's
   settings, edited sounds and sequences survive a restart.
7. **A VST3 target configured** in the build, built and loaded once in a host to show it works, not
   released.
8. **`docs/reference/ks32-front-panel.md` revised**: Figure 2 as the layout source, the observed
   button codes and LED numbers, and the photo for orientation only (umbrella §5).

## 3. What "passed" means

The user's bar, from the umbrella design: the user starts the app, plays the factory presets from the
Q25, and uses the on-screen panel the way they would use a real KS-32. In particular:

- the app boots to "ROM   00 / KS Grand Piano" with the Select Sound LED lit;
- sounds can be chosen from the panel (Bank, Screen, Up/Yes, Down/No) and from the Q25;
- a two-button chord works: the version screen (Edit Seq/Preset, then hold Bank 9 and press
  Screen 1) shows "OS Version 3.01";
- a setting changed on the panel (for example Master Tune) is still there after closing and
  reopening the app;
- no audible dropouts at the default buffer during a few minutes of playing.

Supporting evidence the builder delivers:

- engine tests in `ens_tests` (both toolchains): event ordering, chord timing, the snapshot, the
  bounded queue, resampled output length, and that the engine renders the same samples as
  `Machine::runFrame` plus `Resampler` called directly;
- a test that fails if `runFrame` and the event calls allocate once the machine is running (§7);
- the decisions log records each judgment call, as before.

## 4. Non-goals (this plan)

- **No VST3 release.** It is configured and smoke-tested only (goal 7). Plugin state, host
  automation and multiple instances are the next stage.
- **No MIDI out.** The firmware's MIDI out is still counted and not sent anywhere. The mockup's MIDI
  "Output" menu is dropped (*my call*: it would need a byte-stream-to-message parser and nothing
  in the user's bar needs it).
- **No memory card.** Card detect stays "empty" (K10).
- **No sustain pedal, CV pedal or pressure on screen.** The Q25 sends sustain and pressure as MIDI,
  which the firmware already takes. How the local pedal jacks reach the firmware was not found in
  Plan 6.
- **No computer-keyboard playing** (the user left it for later).
- **No other platforms.** Windows only, like `ks32emu play`.

## 5. Anti-goals

As in the umbrella design (§5), and these in particular:

- **JUCE in the emulation.** The engine and everything under it stay JUCE-free and OS-free.
- **The GUI guessing what the firmware shows.** The GUI never draws a display character, LED or
  button state of its own making. A control that does nothing on the firmware side looks pressable
  but is marked (§6.3).
- **Copying the photo.** Layout from Figure 2 and the artboard; the photo only for where Figure 2
  is silent (the tracks and transport section).
- **Ensoniq branding.** The app says "KS-32". No Ensoniq logo or trade dress.

## 6. The parts

### 6.1 The machine changes (goal 1)

Make the run loop and the event calls allocation-free once the machine has been created:

- the DUART's receive and transmit buffers and the Panel's byte queues become fixed-capacity rings;
  `takeMidiOut`/`takeToCpu` hand bytes out without swapping vectors;
- the MIDI receive queue is bounded. *My call:* 1024 bytes, about a third of a second of MIDI at the
  wire rate; when full, new bytes are dropped and counted (the counter is readable);
- `PanelState` becomes fixed-size (character arrays, not `std::vector<std::string>`), so a copy is
  a plain copy.

The bus trace (`onBusEvent`) is a debug feature and may allocate when it is switched on. Behaviour
must not change: the Plan 6 hashes (boot trace `d2afd0fed8861cbd`, render `2bae46b3acc34cd0`, smoke
render `a479ba867298cc55`) stay the same on both builds.

### 6.2 The engine (goal 2)

One class, *my call* `ens::app::Engine`, in its own JUCE-free library. It has two sides:

- **The audio side** (one thread at a time; the audio callback):
  - `render(float* l, float* r, int frames)` takes the waiting events, applies them, and runs the
    machine through the resampler until `frames` are ready;
  - a MIDI byte reaches the machine at its sample offset within the block, not at the block start
    (`ks32emu play` quantises to the block; *my call*: the app does better, because it is cheap
    here);
  - after each block it publishes the panel snapshot (display rows, blink mask, LEDs) and a few
    counters (MIDI bytes dropped, frames rendered).
  - MIDI comes in through `render`'s arguments: the bytes of the block's `MidiBuffer`, with their
    sample offsets (§6.5), so MIDI never crosses threads inside the engine.
- **The other side** (the GUI):
  - `post(Event)` puts a button, key or analog event on one bounded single-producer queue, written
    on the message thread and read by the audio side. The audio side stamps them at the start of the
    block they are taken in;
  - `snapshot()` returns the latest published panel state, read without a lock (*my call*: a
    sequence counter around a copy, retried if it changed mid-copy).

The engine also owns: the ROM loading (`create` from a `RomSet`), the RAM image save and load, and
the sample-rate change (a new `Resampler`; the machine keeps running). Re-creating the machine
(ROM folder changed, memory erased) happens with audio stopped, from the message thread.

### 6.3 The front-panel editor (goal 4)

- **Layout.** The "Front panel" artboard is the reference: 2040 x 720 design units. The editor draws
  in those units and scales them uniformly to the window, keeping the aspect ratio; the window is
  resizable with a minimum of about half size. *My call:* the default window is 1360 x 480, two
  thirds of the design size. The settings (gear) button sits in the wordmark column, under the
  artboard's "ROM v3.01 / MIDI: Q25" status lines, which show the live ROM version and MIDI input.
- **Colours** as in the artboard: panel #1f2023, display amber #ffb347 on near-black, strip outline
  #5fb3a1, LED on #ff4a3d and off #4a2522. Fonts: the system's condensed sans for labels.
- **The display.** 2 rows of 16 characters from the snapshot, in a monospaced font. Codes 0x20-0x7E
  are drawn as ASCII. Codes 0x00-0x1F and 0x7F have no known glyphs yet (Medium confidence that they
  are characters, interface doc line 309): *my call*, they are drawn as a blank cell, and a debug
  overlay (toggled from a menu) shows their codes in hex so their meaning can be found by
  watching. Blink positions (the blink mask) blink.
- **LEDs** from the snapshot: off, on or blinking.
- **Blink rate.** Not measured. *My call:* 2 Hz, 50 % duty, for both display and LEDs, logged as
  a decision to revisit.
- **Buttons** send press on mouse down and release on mouse up. **Chords:** a right-click (or
  Shift-click) latches a button down; it stays held, drawn pressed, until it is clicked again. That
  makes Bank 9 + Screen 1 two clicks with the mouse. Every button also gets a tooltip naming it.
- **Record, Stop/Continue and Play** have no codes yet. The plan tries to find them the Plan 6 way
  (send each code the firmware has not been seen to use, from a sequencer screen, and watch the
  display; no ROM code is read). *My call on its bounds:* it is one task, run headless with
  `ks32emu` on a fresh RAM image (never the user's memory file), covering the press codes not in
  `Button` once each from the sequencer screen. Codes found go into `Button` and the interface doc.
  "Not found" is an acceptable result and does not block the plan: any still unknown are drawn
  dimmed, do nothing, and say so in their tooltip.
- **Sliders.** Volume and Data Entry are vertical drag controls that call `setAnalog` with 0-0x3FF.
  They start at the K13 resting values (Volume at maximum, Data Entry at mid).
- **Wheels.** Pitch springs back to centre (0x200) on mouse up; Mod stays where it is left. Both
  drag vertically.
- **Keys.** The 76 keys send `key(note, down, strike)`. The strike comes from where the key is
  clicked: near the front edge is loud, near the back is soft, 1-127 linear along the key length
  (*my call*). Dragging across keys plays them in turn (glissando). Notes arriving on MIDI are not
  drawn on the keyboard (the firmware does not report them back to the panel).
- **Accessibility basics.** Each control has a name for the screen reader and keyboard focus; Space
  presses the focused button.
- **Before the machine runs** (no ROMs found, or a ROM file of the wrong size), the display shows
  the reason in the same amber, for example "ROM missing:" / "sq1-u26.bin", and a button opens the
  settings page.

### 6.4 The settings page (goal 5)

As the "Settings" artboard shows it, opened from a gear button on the panel, with these changes:

- **ROMs.** The folder field and "Choose folder…" button, and the list of the six files
  (`ks32/ks32v301upper.bin`, `ks32/ks32v301lower.bin`, `sq1-u25.bin`, `sq1-u26.bin`, `rom2.u39`,
  `rom3.u38`), each marked found, missing or wrong size. Choosing a folder re-creates the machine
  (keeping the memory) when all six are good.
- **MIDI and audio.** *My call:* one `AudioDeviceSelectorComponent` on the Standalone wrapper's
  device manager (§6.5), styled to match, showing the audio output, buffer size and MIDI inputs (no
  MIDI output, §4), rather than our own menus. The wrapper saves and reopens the devices itself.
  Under it, the line "The KS-32 runs at 29 762 Hz and is resampled to <rate> Hz" and an underrun
  count. In the VST3 build this section is hidden: the host owns the devices.
- **Memory.** The memory file and "Change…", the note, and "Erase memory…". Erase asks for
  confirmation, then loads an all-zero image, which makes the firmware rebuild its memory on the
  next boot. *My call:* the default file is `ks32-memory.bin` in the app's data folder
  (`%APPDATA%\KS-32\`), not next to the ROMs.
- The ROM folder and memory file are kept in a properties file in the same folder, shared by the
  Standalone and VST3 builds.

### 6.5 The app shell (goal 3)

- **JUCE's stock Standalone wrapper** (*my call*), not a custom application: `juce_add_plugin` with
  the formats Standalone and VST3, one `AudioProcessor` that owns the engine, and the same processor
  and editor in both builds. MIDI arrives in `processBlock` as a `MidiBuffer` in both builds and is
  handed to the engine with its sample offsets. The editor reaches the wrapper's device manager
  through `StandalonePluginHolder` only in the Standalone build. The wrapper's own Options menu
  is left in place.
- **Start-up.** Load the settings, load the ROMs and the memory file, create the engine, open the
  devices, start audio. The firmware boots in real time, as the real instrument does: the display
  shows the start-up screens for about two seconds before the play screen.
- **Shut-down.** Stop audio, save the memory image from the engine, save the settings.
- **Crash safety.** *My call:* the memory is also saved every 60 s while running, to a temporary
  file that replaces the old one, so a crash loses at most a minute of edits.
- **Two warnings to know about** (firmware behaviour, from Plan 6, documented for the user in the
  app's readme, not worked around): OS v3.01 always powers on to ROM 00; and six or so start-ups of
  under about 2 s each make the firmware reinitialize its memory. The app always runs the boot to
  the end before a save, so a normal quick open and close does not trigger it. *My call:* the app
  does not save memory if it closes before the play screen has been reached.

### 6.6 The build (goals 3 and 7)

- JUCE 8 is fetched by CMake (`FetchContent`) at a pinned release tag, *my call* the newest JUCE 8
  tag at the time the plan is written, recorded in the plan. **Fetching it is a download and needs
  the user's permission** when the plan reaches it.
- An option `ENS_BUILD_APP` (default OFF) adds the app, so the MinGW build and the existing test
  runs never need JUCE. The app builds with MSVC x64 in `build-msvc/`.
- `juce_add_plugin` with the formats Standalone and VST3. The engine library and its tests build
  without the option, on both toolchains.
- JUCE's licence (AGPLv3 or commercial) is noted in the readme, as the umbrella design says.

## 7. Real-time rules

The audio callback must not allocate, lock or wait. To make that checkable rather than hoped:

- the engine's `render` path is covered by a test that counts allocations (*my call*: a global
  `operator new` replacement in that test executable only, counting between a start and a stop
  call) and requires zero after the machine has booted;
- the GUI reads only the published snapshot, never the machine;
- the bus trace is never switched on in the app.

## 8. Decisions this plan expects to log

New entries in `docs/decisions.md`, under a new heading for the app, for at least: the MIDI queue
bound, the blink rate, the glyphs for 0x00-0x1F, the strike-from-click mapping, the chord latch, any
Record/Stop/Play codes found (or why not), and the memory-save rules in §6.5.
